5. Three worked examples, step by step

Syrian Arab Republic: catastrophic war, 2012

  1. What went in. Syrian Arab Republic's income per person was $1,543 in 2011. Over the 10 years before, it grew +2.0% a year on average, swinging between -1.8% and +5.1%. The war: 275 battle deaths per 100,000 people a year for 5 years (Syria at its worst, 2012–2016).
  2. The no-war forecast. A time-series model fitted to Syrian Arab Republic's own history (ARIMA(0,1,0) with drift) expects about +0.9% growth a year, reaching $1,695 by 2022 (90% range $1,210–$2,369).
  3. What the war adds. In past wars this intense, income per person ended up -24% after 1 year, -38% after 5 years and -29% after 10, compared with no war.
  4. Final answer. With the war: about $1,197 by 2022 (90% range $469–$2,345), i.e. 29% poorer than without it.
  5. What really happened. By 2022, income per person was $717: -40% vs. our with-war prediction and -58% vs. the no-war forecast.
  6. Note: Syrian Arab Republic was already in conflict the year before, so this is modeled as a new war on top of that.

6. Run it yourself (Windows)

Open two PowerShell windows.

Window 1: the engine (API)

cd C:\Users\ggrea\cdc2026\CDC26\backend
.venv\Scripts\python -m uvicorn conflict_sim.api.app:app --port 8000

Check it at http://localhost:8000/docs. First time on a new machine, set up the environment first:

cd C:\Users\ggrea\cdc2026\CDC26\backend
python -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\python -m pip install -e ".[dev]"
.venv\Scripts\python scripts\fetch_wb_dev.py        # dev data pull (~1 min)
.venv\Scripts\python -m conflict_sim.estimation     # rebuild estimates (~10 min)
.venv\Scripts\python -m conflict_sim.backtest       # scorecard used by the page

Window 2: the explorer page

cd C:\Users\ggrea\cdc2026\CDC26\frontend
npm ci                      # first time only
npx vite --port 5180

Then open http://localhost:5180/explorer.html. Port 5180 is used because 5173 is taken by another app on this machine.
